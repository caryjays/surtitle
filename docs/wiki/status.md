## Where things stand

Direction is set in the [roadmap](roadmap.md): a presenter-controlled caption layer pairing English with one other language per session. Japanese is the first pair and Spanish a pilot; French, Italian and Portuguese have profiles and gates in the pilot's shape. Chinese has a profile in Simplified and Traditional (Taiwan), and Korean a profile; each has a gate with one run, and the local translator has been read through and timed in both pairs. Other languages no longer wait for the Japanese [gate](gates.md); each ships by passing its own.

What runs:

- Local Turbo Q4 recognition. Rolling segmentation is the default: it decodes every second, locks text two decodes agree on, and by default shows text three decodes agree on before it locks. Turns mode remains as an option.
- Three translation profiles: off (the default), offline (`pnpm dev:offline`, a pinned local Qwen3.5-4B with streaming replies) and cloud (`pnpm dev:listen`).
- The operator console at `/control`, with an audience view, overlay URL builder, health, shortcuts, runtime settings and a switch between the pairs while capture is off; the roll-up overlay with a reading-pace pacer; opt-in content-free diagnostics read with `pnpm diag:latency`.
- Speech outside the pair is not captioned and leaves a marker row. A recognizer that falls behind restarts on its own, visibly.
- A standalone project homepage in `site/` introduces the project, links to GitHub and the wiki, and explains local captions, offline translation and OBS setup. GitHub Pages serves it from `main`; see [the homepage entry](../log/2026-11-22-project-homepage.md) and [its publication](../log/2026-11-22-homepage-on-pages.md).

The maintainer has rehearsed it in OBS with English, Japanese and off-pair speech, and has run microphone sessions in `en-es` with offline translation; see [other languages](../log/2026-11-10-other-languages.md) and [the session](../log/2026-11-12-en-es-microphone-session.md).

## Translation quality

The maintainer does not read the gate languages, so an agent session grades the offline profile by reading whole held-out talks; the method is in [gates](gates.md#translation-read-through). In the first read-through, long complete sentences translate well in all four directions and the mean grade on correctly recognized rows runs from 3.8 to 4.3 of 5, English to Japanese lowest on jargon-heavy earnings calls. The losses are subjects the 4B model gets wrong where the source language leaves them out, terms and names that change within a talk, and recognition errors translated faithfully. Rewording how the translator uses its context fixed few of them. See [the read-through](../log/2026-11-14-translation-read-through.md). Read-throughs of French and Portuguese put both into English above part 4's mean, at 4.58 and 4.67, missing only on a few low rows, and both out of English below it or at its edge, at 4.16 and 4.26, with domain terms on the earnings calls and errors of gender and agreement; see [the entry](../log/2026-11-19-host-probe-and-read-throughs.md). Chinese and Korean are in [the translator series](../log/2026-11-22-chinese-korean-local-translation.md): Chinese to English at 4.44, English to Chinese at 4.12, Korean to English at 3.98, and English to Korean at 3.46 with its Japanese openings. Translated again over the same recognized text with term sets and the script guard, English to Korean reads at 3.98; see [the guard](../log/2026-11-25-korean-script-guard.md).

## Chinese

Roadmap stage 3 has started. `zh` has a profile with two scripts, Simplified and Traditional in Taiwan's character forms, chosen per session as a pair subtag (`en-zh-Hans`, `en-zh-Hant`) and switchable on the operator page while capture is off. The service converts recognized Chinese and translations into Chinese to the session's script with OpenCC; recognized text is converted before rolling compares decodes, since Whisper changes script between decodes. The [Mandarin gate](gates.md#mandarin-gate) has public and held-out sets, swept stock phrases with a pattern for subtitle credits, and scoring that folds the two scripts. Its one rolling run passes every measured part but held-out error over one-pass, which fails narrowly on lectures joined without pauses; the rated share of major rows is not measured. Cantonese is recognized as Chinese and shown. The local translator passes the offline browser smoke in both scripts, and part 6 passes on every talk in `en-zh-Hant`. In the read-through, Chinese to English grades above part 4's mean but invents more than its 2%, mostly where misheard English terms meet an anecdote. English to Chinese grades a little lower, and the Traditional output uses mainland vocabulary in places. A prompt note asking for Taiwan's words changed the grade by nothing under a blind read, and OpenCC's phrase conversion rewrote ordinary words wrongly; neither is in ([the log](../log/2026-11-25-korean-script-guard.md)). A term's `zh-Hant` form in a term set is the way to fix a word. No microphone session has run in the pair. See [the scripts](../log/2026-11-21-chinese-scripts.md) and [the gate run](../log/2026-11-21-mandarin-gate.md).

## Korean

Roadmap stage 4 has started. `ko` has a profile scored by character and written with spaces, so text is paced in whole words; its reading rates are a guess. The worker's check that a retried decode is in the pair language now reads each language's letters from its profile; before, Hangul failed it. The [Korean gate](gates.md#korean-gate) has public and held-out sets, swept stock phrases with a pattern for subtitle credits, and one rolling run that passes every measured part; the rated share of major rows is not measured. The held-out talks are fragments joined with even 0.5 s pauses, which suit rolling better than a real talk. In the read-through of 2026-11-22, English to Korean was the weakest direction read: every talk opened in Japanese for its first rows, from the English-to-Japanese example glossary then sent in every pair, and Chinese slipped into later rows. Term sets ended the first: a pair receives only names and terms with a form in its own languages ([the log](../log/2026-11-23-glossary-term-sets.md)). A script guard ended the second: with the direction known, the local translator cannot generate a Chinese character or kana into Korean. A translation into Korean is also shown with each particle joined to the number or Latin word before it, which the model leaves apart. Over the same recognized text, the six English talks read at a mean of 3.98 on clean rows, from 3.52 under the same judge, with rows at 2 or lower down from 18.1% to 5.2%; rows that were already Korean did not move ([the guard](../log/2026-11-25-korean-script-guard.md)). That is level with Korean to English and under part 4's mean. What remains is a call's central term rendered several ways, speech level that drops out of the formal in some rows, domain words taken literally, and a space the model leaves before a counter, `2 월`, which Korean allows. The offline smoke passes in `en-ko`. Korean to English loses subjects that Korean leaves out. Part 6 passes on every talk in a repeat with the guard, under lighter load than the first run, in which one English talk missed narrowly. No microphone session has run in the pair. See [the run](../log/2026-11-22-korean-gate.md) and [the translator series](../log/2026-11-22-chinese-korean-local-translation.md).

## Known limits

- The held-out Japanese talk with frequent pauses. Its loss is fragmentation into short stretches, which rolling's short windows decode badly; nothing tested cut it by half. The closing decode makes it slightly worse in most runs.
- A change of language inside a row without a pause: when the first language's decode of the second stays confident, the rest of the row shows the new speech as text in the old language. The maintainer decided not to build detection inside a row for now.
- Overlapping talkers and far-field group speech are not supported. A microphone near the speaker is the supported setup.

## Open problems, ranked

1. Faint speech in rooms. Field rejections in breakout sessions came mostly from the speech gate with the energy gate at its floor. A sweep on clean clips did not reproduce them: recognition holds down to 24 dB under a normal level, and below that the energy gate stops rows from opening. A room's noise under faint speech is untested. The operator page warns when speech-like sound stays under the noise gate.
2. Recognizer failures under load. Each automatic restart reloads the model and loses the speech during it. Whether to drop the oldest queued final instead is open.
3. Translation quality of the local 4B model. It picks the wrong subject where Spanish, Italian, Japanese or Korean leaves it out, with the preceding rows in front of it, and terms and names change within a talk. A reworded context instruction fixed about a fifth of the low rows on two talks. Left for now by the maintainer's call. Candidates when it is taken up, each testable by re-translating the stored transcripts: a larger local model, a term list kept during the talk. Into Korean the model writes a name in Hangul even with the name mapped to itself, so a name needs its Hangul spelling under `heard` to stay as written. Writing in the wrong language inside a Korean reply is ended by the script guard; a call's central term still takes several renderings, which a term set for the talk addresses. A row that is itself an instruction, such as a speaker quoting a prompt injection, is never obeyed, but with context before it the model repeats the earlier rows' translations in its place; the translator's retry without context catches an echoed source, not this ([the review](../log/2026-11-23-language-pair-review.md)).
4. Translation delay: part 6 is met or accepted in every pair; three talks in Spanish and Italian still miss narrowly. A length close that takes the fresh decode's text exists as the experiment `--fresh-length`. In one Italian run it lowered p90 on 6 of 8 talks, which is within the spread between replays. It needs a repeat in the other order and a read of row ends ([the comparison](../log/2026-11-20-obs-live-path-and-fresh-length.md)). See [translation latency](../log/2026-11-11-translation-latency.md).
5. Proper names and product names are misrecognized in several spellings. A name's `heard` spellings in a term set are replaced in recognized text, which fixes the ones listed; not yet tried on a microphone. The vocabulary prompt remains a candidate for the rest, with silence checks.
6. Turns mode: first text waits up to 12 s on continuous speech, and a third-language final still shows as `unknown`. It loses to rolling on every held-out measure; whether to retire it is open.
7. The rule that keeps agreed text out of the middle of a word, and the one that restores a space Whisper omits between segments, now read accented letters and Hangul as letters; unit-tested, and not measured on talks in either state ([the log](../log/2026-11-23-word-boundaries-and-smoke.md)).
8. Minor: snapshots are serialized once per client per tick; a pause that exceeds its drain window reports "recognition unavailable"; torch is installed only for a 2 MB VAD model.

## Standing priorities

- Local recognition stays a hard requirement. Cloud recognition is not a substitute.
- Live defaults stay frozen until a controlled comparison shows a benefit. Recognition tuning is paused. Timing work is judged by speech to translated text, which part 6 gates.
- No paid calls, audience recording, remote exposure or pushing to the public repository without explicit authorization.
- The OBS presentation stack comes after the personal companion is reliable.
