# Rolling step 4: locked-text marker, reading-pace release and roll-up overlay

2026-11-06. The display half of the [rolling recognition design](2026-10-26-rolling-recognition-design.md) is built: step 4 of its plan. Rows carry how much of their text is locked, each page releases text at a reading pace, and the overlay is a roll-up window. Recognition is unchanged apart from what a partial event carries. Rolling stays behind `LOCAL_SEGMENTATION=rolling`.

## What changed

- The rolling adapter's partial events now carry the row's locked text plus the newest decode's unlocked tail, with `stable` set to the locked length. A row shows no tail before its first lock, or while a lock after a noise trim waits for a confident decode, so a hallucinated tail on music never reaches even the operator. `Segment.stable` carries the count to the browser, and a final row drops it.
- `apps/web/pacer.ts` releases text in each client, as designed: originals and translations in separate lanes, a 4 s drain held between per-language rates from the new `reading` field of each profile, and a gradual catch-up. English is released in whole words.
- The overlay shows only released text, in a bottom-anchored window of about four translation lines whose earlier lines slide up. The old two- and three-line clamps are gone: they cut off the end of a long caption, which is its newest text.
- The control page shows released text normally and everything else lighter: locked text not yet released, and the unlocked tail. A "Pace text at reading speed" setting, on by default, turns pacing off there.
- `/overlay` accepts a query of display settings, `lines=1..8` and `pace=off`, so the Browser Source URL configures it. Every other path still matches exactly, and the overlay's query is limited to 100 characters of lowercase letters, digits, `=` and `&`.

## Departure from the design

The design had the rate drain the backlog over 4 s. Recomputed every tick, the rate shrinks with the backlog, so each row's last characters crawl out at the floor rate. A 20-character Japanese row took over 5 s. The rate now rises with new text and is held until the lane is empty, so a row drains in about the drain time.

## Checks

- `pnpm check`: 135 tests, including 7 pacer tests with scripted timings: steady release with no burst above the ceiling, whole-word English, Japanese rates, locked text never rewritten and the tail never released, a 650-character backlog sped up gradually and drained in under 26 s, independent translation lanes, and clear, new-session and disconnect resets. A rolling adapter test now checks that the locked prefix never retracts and that tails are sent; a server test checks the overlay query rules.
- `pnpm test:browser`: passes. Its check that heartbeats cause no caption DOM writes now first waits for paced text to finish releasing. A repeated write of the history's empty-state attribute was removed on the way.
- `pnpm test:local-browser` and `pnpm test:listen-browser`, turns mode with the real model: pass.
- New `pnpm test:rolling-browser`: the listen smoke in rolling mode with the real model. Partial rows carried tails, and the overlay never showed unreleased text. Pass.
- A visual check with authored English text and a fake translator showed the overlay releasing words and characters and rolling earlier lines up, and the control page showing the lighter rest.

## Open

- Rates, drain and window size are design values, not tuned. The maintainer's rehearsal in listen mode with rolling on, step 5, decides them, along with whether the original keeps its own lane above the translation and whether rolling becomes the default.
- Paced translations add their release time to the roughly 2 s translation round trip. The Japanese translation of a long English row reads out at 3 to 10 characters per second.
- A row whose original has released nothing yet does not show its translation on the overlay.
