# Display simulations and the operator console

2026-11-06. A display simulator runs authored sessions through the pacer in virtual time, and `/control` is rebuilt as an operator console with a reader mode. Two service settings, segmentation and translation, can now change at runtime from the console. Recognition and the overlay's behavior are unchanged.

## Display simulations

`pnpm sim:display` runs fictional English and Japanese sessions through `apps/web/pacer.ts` in 100 ms steps. Speech runs at 14 English or 7 Japanese characters per second. Clause-sized segments lock on the next decode at least 1 s after they end, plus one more decode. Translations arrive 1.2 to 2.5 s after a row's final. Lag is measured from when each character was spoken; a translation counts from the end of its sentence. Timing is modeled, not decoded, so these are development numbers.

| Scenario | Original lag p50 / p90 | Translation lag p50 / p90 | Most characters in one second, original / translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| English lecture | 7.0 / 8.3 s | 7.1 / 9.2 s | 33 / 10 |
| English, fast speaker | 6.8 / 7.8 s | 6.7 / 8.8 s | 33 / 10 |
| Japanese lecture | 6.5 / 6.8 s | 6.6 / 8.3 s | 7 / 30 |
| Alternating Q&A | 7.0 / 8.2 s | 6.7 / 8.7 s | 33 / 30 |
| English, translation 3 to 6 s | 7.0 / 8.5 s | 9.6 / 11.9 s | 33 / 10 |
| English, pacing off | 4.6 / 7.2 s | 4.1 / 5.2 s | 102 / 53 |

- Pacing costs about 2.4 s of median original lag on English and caps bursts near the ceiling. 33 characters in a second is the 25/s ceiling plus whole-word rounding; unpaced, 102 characters landed in one second.
- Pacing costs about 3 s on Japanese translations of English. A 45-character Japanese translation takes 4.5 s at the 10/s ceiling, so translation lag is set by the Japanese ceiling, not by the drain.
- A shorter drain mainly helps Japanese originals: a 2 s drain gave 4.5 s median lag against 6.5 s. English p90 and translation lag barely moved, since the ceilings bind there.

These suggest the rehearsal should try a higher Japanese ceiling for translations before a shorter drain. Nothing was changed on these numbers alone.

## Console

`/control` now has:

- a top bar with a capture-state pill, the presets, the capture controls and the input level;
- a status line;
- the transcript;
- a sidebar of panels: Audience view, Input, Session, Overlay for OBS, Health, Diagnostic log, and Help & notes.

Reading focus collapses it to the reader. Highlights:

- The audience view renders the overlay with the overlay's own code, now shared as `apps/web/audience.ts`, on a scaled 1920 × 1080 stage. It opens no extra socket.
- The Overlay for OBS panel builds the Browser Source URL from the window size and pacing, saves them under `captions.overlay.v1`, and copies the URL.
- The Health panel shows content-free status and medians over the last 10 rows.
- Keyboard shortcuts: S start or pause, T Translate now, 1 to 3 directions, R Reading focus, L latest, / search, ? list. They are ignored while typing. Clear screen and New session have none.

## Runtime settings

- `set-segmentation` switches turns and rolling. It is refused while capture is on and applies at the next Start, so a row never mixes the two modes. `LOCAL_SEGMENTATION` is now the starting value. Previews are passed to the adapter in both modes; rolling never uses them.
- `set-translation` switches a configured translator off or on for the session. Off drops queued requests and marks their rows original; a request already sent finishes. The reported processing changes with it. Without a configured translator it cannot be switched on, so paid calls still need the listen profile.
- The snapshot reports `segmentation`, or null for a recognizer without the choice, and `translation` as available and enabled.

## Checks

- `pnpm check`: 139 tests, including four new controller tests for the settings.
- `pnpm test:browser` exercises the audience view, the overlay URL, the settings, shortcuts in and out of text fields, the shortcut list and the health panel. The narrow-width check now measures the column in Reading focus, where the sidebar is gone.
- `pnpm test:rolling-browser` passes against the real model.
- Screenshots at 1440 × 900, in Reading focus and at 390 px wide, with authored text, show the layout holding. The top bar first wrapped at 1440 px; the shortcut button became `?`.

## Open

- The maintainer's rehearsal: console usability, rates, drain and window size, and whether rolling becomes the default.
- The overlay settings in the console are a template for the URL. The running overlay reads only its own URL, so changing the panel does not change an overlay already in OBS until its URL is updated.
